# 4.1 Experimental Settings

## Training Data.

We conduct mid-training (continual pre-training) on open-domain professional corpora. Following the anchor-selection protocol described in Section 3, the primary training corpus is a 1B-token subset sampled from **MegaMath-Web-Pro**. For each document, a frozen proxy model, **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B**, performs a single forward pass to estimate the per-token next-word prediction entropy; documents longer than the proxy's maximum context (4096 tokens) are processed with a half-stride sliding window (stride 2048), and the entropies of overlapping regions are averaged.

From the cached entropy sequence we construct three anchor arms as ablations: (i) **high**, the top-5% highest-entropy tokens of each document (implemented as a coarse 15% screening followed by re-ranking to 5%); (ii) **low**, the bottom-5% lowest-entropy tokens; and (iii) **rand**, 5% of tokens drawn uniformly without replacement (global seed 42, per-document seed $42 + \mathrm{doc\_idx}$). Each anchor induces a training prompt consisting only of the up to 1024 tokens preceding the anchor (the anchor token itself is excluded); a continuation of up to 512 tokens is then generated by the student. To remove redundant anchors whose 1024-token contexts heavily overlap, anchors within the same document are deduplicated over non-overlapping 512-token windows, keeping only the highest-entropy anchor per window. All three arms are down-sampled to the exact same size — 435,049 training samples, matched to the smallest (high) arm — so that the arms differ only in the *selection* of anchoring positions, with identical numbers of training samples and optimization steps. For the coding-domain experiment we additionally construct a parallel corpus from **StarCoderData** (1B tokens) using the same anchor pipeline.

## Implementation Details.

Our implementation is built on the OPD codebase, which extends **veRL v0.7.0** with a **vLLM** rollout engine. The student is **Qwen3-1.7B-Base**, and the teacher is the stronger same-family base model **Qwen3-14B-Base**; both are frozen except for the student's trainable parameters, and the teacher is used only for forward token-probability evaluation. For scaling-law verification we vary the teacher–student pair (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base → Qwen3-8B-Base).

The student performs a single on-policy rollout per anchor ($n=1$) with stochastic sampling (temperature 1.0, top-$p$ 0.95). The distillation signal is the token-level Reverse KL reward $r_i = \log \pi_\psi(y_i \mid s_i) - \log \pi_{\theta}^{(\mathrm{old})}(y_i \mid s_i)$, which we approximate over the top-$K{=}16$ student-probable candidate tokens (`only_stu`) and weight by the normalized teacher probability (`teacher_p`); `token_reward_direct` is used as the advantage estimator, and token losses are aggregated with `token-mean`. An entropy-regularized KL penalty toward the reference model is added (`low_var_kl`, coefficient 0.005) to prevent catastrophic drift. Optimization uses a learning rate of $1\times10^{-6}$ with a 0.03 linear warmup ratio, a mini-batch size of 64 (global batch $64\times N_{\mathrm{GPUs}}$), bfloat16 mixed precision, full parameter fine-tuning under FSDP with gradient checkpointing and activation offloading, and a single training epoch with checkpoints saved every 100 steps. Training is performed on 4–8 NVIDIA A100/A800 80GB GPUs. Validation is disabled during training (`test_freq = -1`) and performed offline after training; for fairness, the NTP baseline is trained on the identical corpus with identical hyper-parameters and hardware, differing only in the training objective (standard next-token prediction vs. on-policy distillation).

## Benchmarks.

We evaluate the resulting models on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, primarily **MATH-500** and **Minerva**, with additional competition-level checks on **AIME25** and **DAPO**. For each benchmark we sample $n=16$ responses per problem with stochastic decoding (temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.95) using the base chat template without thinking tokens, and grade answers by rule-based matching of the boxed final answer. We report **pass@1** (best-of-$n$) and **avg@k** (the average per-problem solve rate over the $n$ samples). For coding ability we additionally report greedy pass@1 on **HumanEvalPlus**, **MBPPPlus**, and **LiveCodeBench**.
